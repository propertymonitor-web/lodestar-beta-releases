LODESTAR  -  THE FAMILY OF MESHCORE FIRMWARE BY FEAR REACTIVE
=============================================================
Overview, how each one differs, how LODESTAR compares with other options,
and answers to common questions.   Status: BETA TEST.   Free and open source.


WHAT IS LODESTAR?
-----------------
LODESTAR is a family of firmware for MeshCore radios. MeshCore is an
open-source system that lets cheap LoRa radios pass text messages from
radio to radio with no cell signal, no Wi-Fi and no monthly fee. LODESTAR
does not replace MeshCore. It is MeshCore with extra brains added for
people who take their radios out of the house: driving, hiking, boating,
camping, and exploring.

You flash it onto a radio you already own (or buy), the same way you would
flash stock MeshCore, using the web flasher at https://flasher.meshcore.io
in Chrome or Edge. Your phone app, your contacts and your channels keep
working as before.

NOVA  -  the smarter companion
------------------------------
Who it is for: anyone who carries a MeshCore companion radio away from home.

Stock MeshCore connects your phone to the mesh. NOVA keeps all of that and
answers the questions you really have while you are out:
  * "Can I reach anyone from here?"   Link screen: the repeaters your radio
    hears right now, ranked best first, with a score and an arrow for
    getting better or worse.
  * "Did my message get through, and who carried it?"   Sent screen: sent,
    heard by repeater 3F, delivered in 4 seconds, or failed.
  * "How far back was the last place I had coverage?"   Back screen: an
    arrow and distance to the last place you had a usable repeater.
  * "How long will the battery last?"   Battery screen: percentage, trend,
    rough time left, and a graph.
  * "Is it worth walking up that hill?"   Range screen: ask nearby
    repeaters to answer and see GOOD / OK / WEAK / NONE for where you stand.
  Also: trip distance and speed, power profiles (hike / car / desk), quiet
  hours (no sound or screen wake at night, favourites still get through),
  and a live dashboard page on your computer with the full detail, a street
  map of your trip, and downloadable logs.
Screens are on the Heltec V4 R2. The XIAO nRF52840 has no screen, so you
use the dashboard instead.
What NOVA does not do: it only listens and keeps score. It does not change
how packets are routed.


ORION  -  the wardriving edition of NOVA
----------------------------------------
Who it is for: people who want to MAP the mesh. Drive or walk with a radio
that has GPS and ORION records every repeater and companion it hears.
  * Green dot on the map = a repeater. Blue square = a companion.
  * Your route is drawn as a line on a real street map.
  * Download the results as a spreadsheet file (CSV), a map file (GeoJSON)
    or a GPS track with points (GPX).
  * Offline maps: download your state (or the Route 66 map) at home and the
    map still works with no signal.
ORION is the same firmware as NOVA with the ORION name and the dashboard
opening in wardriving mode. If mapping is why you bought it, choose ORION.
Without a GPS, only nodes that announce their own position can be placed.
Please treat the logs as private: they contain other people's radio names
and the places they announce.


PULSAR  -  the repeater that moves
----------------------------------
Who it is for: anyone running a repeater that is NOT bolted to a tower: in
a vehicle, on a boat, on foot, or at a camp.

A tower never changes its neighbours. A vehicle gains and loses them all
day. PULSAR keeps a live ranking of the repeaters it can hear, scored on
(1) whether that repeater really passes along what we send, (2) signal
quality, and (3) how few hops its traffic needs. It uses the ranking to:
  * decide WHEN to forward: the best-placed repeater goes first, a weak one
    yields to better-placed neighbours;
  * drop its own queued copy of a packet when a strong uplink already sent
    it, which saves airtime for everyone;
  * avoid flip-flopping between two similar uplinks while you drive;
  * forget repeaters you have driven away from.
It ALWAYS forwards and never silences your node. If it has no good uplink,
it behaves exactly like the stock MeshCore repeater. The command "lodestar"
prints the live ranked table.
Honest limit: MeshCore does not let a repeater hand a packet to one chosen
neighbour, so PULSAR steers by timing and by cancelling duplicates. It also
cannot read your messages (they are encrypted).
A fixed repeater whose neighbours never change gains less from PULSAR.


HEARTH  -  the room server
--------------------------
Who it is for: groups, clubs, camps and neighbourhoods that want a message
board that is always on. A room server is MeshCore's "bulletin board": it
keeps the most recent messages so people who were out of range can read
what they missed when they come back. It is not a repeater (stock MeshCore
advises against making it one).
HEARTH is stock MeshCore room-server firmware for the XIAO ESP32S3 with the
LODESTAR HEARTH name. Be clear about what it is: a ready-made, tested build
with the family branding, not a new routing system.


HELIOS  -  the solar repeater (coming)
--------------------------------------
HELIOS is planned as the solar-powered repeater for the SenseCAP Solar Node
P1-Pro: a repeater you can mount on a pole or a roof and leave. It is in
development and not released. Nothing in this file should be read as a
promise of a date or a feature list.

----------------

  * Seeed Studio XIAO ESP32S3 + Wio-SX1262 kit.

Never power any radio on without its antenna screwed on.


HOW LODESTAR COMPARES
---------------------
vs STOCK MESHCORE FIRMWARE
  Stock MeshCore is the base, and it works well. LODESTAR is stock MeshCore
  plus extras, built on top of it. What you gain:
    - NOVA / ORION: delivery tracking, link ranking, trip and battery
      screens, dashboard with map, quiet hours, power profiles.
    - PULSAR: live repeater ranking, smarter forwarding timing, duplicate
      cancelling, a ranked table.
  What you give up: LODESTAR is a beta from a small team, and it follows
  MeshCore releases a little behind. Stock MeshCore has far more users and
  testers. If you want the most proven build, run stock. If you want the
  extra information and are happy to report problems, run LODESTAR.
  You can always go back to stock by flashing it again.

vs OTHER COMMUNITY MESHCORE BUILDS
  Other people also publish custom MeshCore firmware. LODESTAR's focus is
  being useful while MOVING (link ranking, "get back to coverage", a
  repeater that adapts as its neighbours change) and mapping the mesh
  (ORION). Compare the feature lists and pick what fits your use.

vs MESHTASTIC
  Meshtastic is a different, separate project that uses the same kind of
  radio. The two do not talk to each other, and you choose one firmware per
  radio. From published comparisons (sources below):
    - Roles. Meshtastic uses one node type with optional router/repeater
      settings. MeshCore has distinct roles (companion, repeater, room
      server), and LODESTAR is built around those.
    - Routing. Meshtastic floods channel messages up to a hop limit (3 by
      default, 7 at most), and newer versions learn routes for direct
      messages. MeshCore finds a path with a first flood, then sends later
      messages along that path, and allows up to 64 hops.
    - Quiet network. MeshCore repeaters only forward, and clients do not
      repeat, which people report as using less airtime on big meshes.
    - Store and forward. MeshCore has room servers for missed messages.
    - Where Meshtastic is stronger. It is generally seen as easier to set
      up, has more documentation, and has built-in position sharing,
      sensor telemetry, MQTT, ATAK and Home Assistant support. If you want
      position sharing among friends with the least fuss, Meshtastic is a
      good choice.
  Neither is "better" for everybody. LODESTAR only makes sense if you have
  chosen MeshCore.

vs SATELLITE MESSENGERS, CELL PHONES AND TWO-WAY RADIOS
  A mesh needs other radios nearby, or a repeater in range. A satellite
  messenger works almost anywhere with a view of the sky, at a monthly
  cost, and is the right tool for emergencies. A mesh has no monthly fee
  and is good for groups and for areas with a few repeaters, but it is
  NOT a replacement for a satellite messenger, a phone, or 911.


QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS
---------------------

Q: What do I need to get started?
A: A supported radio with its antenna, a USB data cable, a computer with
   Chrome or Edge, and the firmware file (from the setup zip or the
   releases page). Flash it at https://flasher.meshcore.io, choose "Custom
   Firmware". Each setup zip has a step-by-step setup.txt for beginners.

Q: Does it cost anything?
A: No. The firmware is free and open source. You only pay for the radio.

Q: Will it work with the normal MeshCore phone app?
A: Yes. NOVA and ORION connect to the MeshCore app over Bluetooth as usual.

Q: Do I lose my contacts and settings if I install it?
A: Flashing over the top normally keeps them. Do NOT tick "Erase" unless you
   mean to wipe the radio. Take a backup from the app first, to be safe.

Q: Can I go back to stock MeshCore?
A: Yes. Flash the stock firmware at flasher.meshcore.io the same way.

Q: Which one do I need: NOVA, ORION, PULSAR or HEARTH?
A: One radio, one firmware.
     - A radio your phone connects to: NOVA (or ORION if you want to map).
     - A repeater: PULSAR.
     - A message board for a group: HEARTH.
   You choose per radio. A normal setup is one NOVA companion for you and
   optionally a PULSAR repeater in the car.

Q: What is the difference between NOVA and ORION?
A: The firmware is the same. ORION carries the wardriving name and opens the
   dashboard in wardriving mode. Choose ORION if mapping is the goal.

Q: Do I need a GPS?
A: Not to use the radio. For trip screens, speed, the back-to-coverage
   arrow, and placing nodes you hear on a map, yes. The Heltec V4 R2 can
   take a GPS module. The XIAO boards do not enable GPS in MeshCore.
   PULSAR works fine without GPS.

Q: Does ORION track people?
A: It lists the radios whose adverts it hears, which are broadcasts that
   every MeshCore radio sends openly, and the position they announce about
   themselves. It cannot read messages. Still, a companion usually
   belongs to a person, so keep the logs private and do not publish a list
   of names and places. Know and follow your local laws.

Q: Will PULSAR make my network faster?
A: It aims to reduce wasted airtime and make hand-off between repeaters
   smoother as you move. It cannot create range, and a repeater that never
   moves gains little. Beta testers' drive logs are how it improves.

Q: Is PULSAR a normal repeater for my home or hilltop?
A: It works as a normal MeshCore repeater, but its extra intelligence is
   aimed at repeaters that move. For a fixed site, stock MeshCore is fine.

Q: Does NOVA send anything on its own?
A: Only one thing: the discovery request when YOU use the Range screen.
   Otherwise it listens and keeps score.

Q: Why does the Sent screen say "unheard" for a channel message?
A: MeshCore channel messages have no acknowledgement, so the radio can only
   tell you whether it heard a repeater pass your message on.

Q: Does the dashboard need the internet?
A: To load, the live page does. A copy of the dashboard (dashboard.html in
   the zip) opens from a file. The street map needs internet unless you load
   an offline map. The radio's data goes only from the USB cable to your
   computer; the page does not send it anywhere.

Q: How do offline maps work?
A: Before a trip, open the dashboard's "Offline maps" box, pick your state
   (all 50 are there, plus a Route 66 map), press Download, then "Load a
   map file". Files are 7 to 380 MB. The dashboard keeps them in your
   browser and switches to them when there is no signal. The maps come from
   OpenStreetMap data built by Protomaps, because the normal online map
   servers do not allow bulk downloading.

Q: Which browsers work?
A: Chrome or Edge on a computer, for flashing and for the dashboard. Firefox
   and Safari cannot talk to USB radios.

Q: Does it work outside the USA?
A: Yes. The EU builds use EU/UK settings and km/h. Always set the radio
   region and frequency to what is legal and agreed on in your own area.
   The EU/UK presets in the beta have not all been verified in the field.

Q: I have a Heltec V4-R8. Can I use it?
A: Not with these builds. They are for the R2 only because the pins for GPS
   and power differ. Do not flash an R2 build on an R8.

Q: Is it tested? Can I trust it?
A: It is a beta. The logic is covered by automated tests on every code
   change, and testers have run NOVA (including speed readout) and HEARTH
   on real radios. Some boards (the XIAO and SenseCAP builds) are newer and
   have had less real-world use. It is not something to bet your life on.
   Bug reports and drive logs are welcome.

Q: Can I rely on it in an emergency?
A: No. A radio mesh is a backup, not a replacement for 911, a phone, or a
   satellite messenger. It only works if other radios or repeaters are
   within range.

Q: Is it affiliated with MeshCore or Meshtastic?
A: No. LODESTAR is an independent project by FEAR REACTIVE that builds on
   the open-source MeshCore firmware. MeshCore and Meshtastic are their own
   projects.

Q: When is HELIOS (the solar repeater) coming?
A: There is no date. It is in development for the SenseCAP Solar Node
   P1-Pro. Follow the project page for news.

Q: Where do I get updates and get help?
A: NOVA and ORION: https://github.com/propertymonitor-web/lodestar_nova_alpha
   PULSAR and HEARTH: https://github.com/propertymonitor-web/lodestar.alpha2
   Find releases under "Releases" and report problems under "Issues".
   Dashboard: https://propertymonitor-web.github.io/lodestar_nova_alpha/
